\section*{Context}

This note follows the previous simulator summary by updating the pseudo-time control component. The base model remains the same: a disentangled VAE maps log-normalized expression into latent space, and a latent diffusion model samples new latents before VAE decoding. The updated runs use whitening-recoloring affine interpolation to generate trajectories with known ground-truth pseudo-time and topology, then use those generated datasets to benchmark trajectory-inference (TI) methods.

\section*{Pseudo-time Interpolation}

Let \(z=(z_{\mathrm{bio}}, z_{\mathrm{tech}})\), where \(z_{\mathrm{bio}}\) contains biological and residual latent dimensions and \(z_{\mathrm{tech}}\) contains technical blocks such as batch. Given start and end state latents with empirical moments \((\mu_0,\Sigma_0)\) and \((\mu_1,\Sigma_1)\), we define an affine endpoint map in the biological subspace,
\[
T(z)=\mu_1+B(z-\mu_0),
\qquad
B=\Sigma_1^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2},
\]
and generate a pseudo-time path by sample-linear interpolation,
\[
z_{\mathrm{bio}}(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)z_{\mathrm{bio}}+\alpha T(z_{\mathrm{bio}}),
\qquad \alpha\in[0,1].
\]
The full latent vector \(\tilde z_\alpha=(z_{\mathrm{bio}}(\alpha),z_{\mathrm{tech}})\) is decoded to expression space, and the known control value \(\alpha\) is recorded as ground-truth pseudo-time. The same control API also supports the Gaussian OT/Bures affine map, but all updated trajectory and TI experiments reported here use whitening-recoloring.

Branching trajectories are built by composing affine paths between observed states. In the pancreas setting used here, \(A=\) Ductal, \(W=\) Ngn3-high endocrine progenitor, and the two terminal states are Beta and Alpha. For branch point \(\tau\), the trunk \(A\rightarrow W\) is assigned \(t=\tau\alpha\), and each daughter path \(W\rightarrow B\) or \(W\rightarrow C\) is assigned \(t=\tau+(1-\tau)\alpha\). This gives each generated cell a known \texttt{true\_pseudotime}, \texttt{true\_lineage}, and \texttt{true\_branch\_point}.

Three knobs control trajectory difficulty:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Endpoint discrepancy}: scale each terminal mean displacement away from \(W\), preserving terminal covariance. Larger discrepancy produces more separated branches.
  \item \textbf{Branch-point position \(\tau\)}: move the split from an early root split toward a late split with a longer shared trunk.
  \item \textbf{Noise scale \(\sigma\)}: add isotropic latent noise to lower the branch signal-to-noise ratio.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Interpolation results.} The linear Ductal-to-Beta experiment is a sanity check: generated cells follow a smooth ordering between known endpoint states (Figure~\ref{fig:pt-control}a). A dose-response check over \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) shows increasing separation between generated pseudo-time groups: ASW rises from approximately \(0.000\) to \(0.238\), while LISI falls from \(1.92\) to \(1.00\) (Figure~\ref{fig:pt-control}b). For branching, changing \(\tau\) visibly moves the split along the common pseudo-time axis: larger \(\tau\) creates a longer trunk and shorter branches (Figure~\ref{fig:pt-control}c).

\section*{TI Methods Benchmarking}

The benchmark exports each generated expression matrix with standardized ground-truth metadata and simulator settings. Method adapters then write a common per-cell output table with \texttt{method}, \texttt{inferred\_pseudotime}, \texttt{inferred\_lineage}, and method metadata. This isolates dependencies: Scanpy DPT/PAGA runs in Python, while Slingshot and Monocle3 are called through experiment-level R adapters.

The updated benchmark uses 2,000 genes, 21 pseudo-time grid values, 100 cells per grid value, and 3 replicates per difficulty setting. All methods use common preprocessing where possible: PCA with 30 components, a 15-neighbor graph, and Leiden/Louvain clustering at resolution 0.5. Evaluation uses global Spearman correlation between inferred and true pseudo-time for ordering recovery, and adjusted Rand index (ARI) between inferred and true lineages for branch/topology recovery.

\textbf{Endpoint discrepancy sweep.} Increasing terminal discrepancy from \(0.2\) to \(1.4\) makes ordering easier for graph-based DPT/PAGA: mean global Spearman rises from \(0.480\) to \(0.874\), and lineage ARI rises from \(0.002\) to \(0.158\). Monocle3 also improves at high discrepancy (\(0.569\) to \(0.817\) Spearman) but still has near-zero lineage ARI. Slingshot is strongest at the lowest discrepancy values (\(0.584\) at \(0.2\), \(0.521\) at \(0.4\)) and more variable at higher discrepancy.

\textbf{Branch-point sweep.} Scanpy DPT/PAGA is the most stable through intermediate branch points, with mean Spearman \(0.806\), \(0.852\), and \(0.853\) at \(\tau=0\), \(0.25\), and \(0.5\). At a late split (\(\tau=0.75\)), Slingshot slightly leads in ordering (\(0.751\) versus \(0.749\) for DPT/PAGA) but has poor lineage ARI. Topology recovery remains harder than ordering: DPT/PAGA gives the best ARI through \(\tau=0.5\) (\(0.057\) to \(0.160\)), while Monocle3 and Slingshot are less reliable.

\textbf{Noise sweep.} Noise gives the cleanest stress test. DPT/PAGA is best through moderate noise, with mean Spearman \(0.806\), \(0.817\), and \(0.725\) at \(\sigma=0\), \(0.5\), and \(1.0\). At \(\sigma=1.5\), Monocle3 is highest (\(0.534\)) while DPT/PAGA drops to \(0.469\). At \(\sigma=2.0\), all methods are weak and high-variance: Slingshot, DPT/PAGA, and Monocle3 average \(0.239\), \(0.189\), and \(0.146\), respectively. This confirms that the simulator generates a controlled difficulty ladder where method performance degrades with lower signal-to-noise ratio.

\section*{Takeaways}
\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}

Pseudo-time control is now more than a visualization device: it defines generated datasets with known ordering, lineage, branch point, and difficulty settings. The first benchmark indicates that recovering a monotone pseudo-time coordinate is easier than recovering branching topology. Scanpy DPT/PAGA is the most stable method in these runs, Slingshot is more variable, and Monocle3 can recover ordering but does not currently produce reliable lineage labels under the adapter/evaluation setup. The next improvement should add richer topology metrics, especially branch-point localization and categorical outcomes such as correct bifurcation, unresolved linear path, or wrong branch structure.
