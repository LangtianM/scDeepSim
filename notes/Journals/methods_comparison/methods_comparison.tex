\noindent\textbf{Comparison framework.}
The comparison separates how a method represents the data-generating process,
how it produces new cells, the domain in which a user can control the output,
and the basis of the variables available for benchmarking.  It characterises
method workflows and control semantics rather than empirical simulation
fidelity.

\begin{description}
  \item[Independent generation.]
  Produces new expression profiles without encoding or transforming a matched
  observed cell at generation time.  This definition is independent of whether
  the profiles arise from specified dynamics, a fitted statistical model, or a
  learned neural distribution.

  \item[Model basis.]
  \emph{Mechanistic} methods specify biological rules or reaction dynamics;
  \emph{fitted statistical} methods estimate an explicit parametric model from
  reference data; and \emph{deep learned} methods train a neural generative
  model on the reference population.

  \item[Generation workflow.]
  \emph{Forward simulation} propagates specified dynamics.  \emph{Parametric
  sampling} draws from a user-specified statistical model, whereas
  \emph{fitted-distribution sampling} draws from a statistical model estimated
  from reference data.  \emph{Learned-distribution sampling} draws new cells
  from a neural model of the population.  \emph{Posterior reconstruction}
  encodes an observed cell before sampling or decoding its reconstruction, and
  \emph{source-cell transformation} applies a learned change to observed input
  cells.

  \item[Control domain.]
  A \emph{parameter sweep} changes simulator parameters;
  \emph{design/covariate specification} sets experimental-design variables;
  \emph{label selection} chooses among learned observed conditions;
  \emph{reference conditioning} anchors output to observed cells or their
  covariates; \emph{contrast transfer} applies a difference learned between
  observed conditions; and a \emph{continuous strength sweep} varies the
  numerical magnitude of a named intervention.

  \item[Control and benchmark basis.]
  \emph{Mechanism-defined} variables follow the simulated dynamics;
  \emph{design-defined} variables follow a specified or fitted experimental
  design; \emph{observed-label-defined} controls use annotations present in the
  reference data; \emph{contrast-defined} controls derive from a learned
  difference between observed conditions; and \emph{intervention-defined}
  variables are assigned by a parameterised operation and are therefore known
  by construction.
\end{description}
